F-6 Rebuild & Renovation

In F-6, You Are Almost Always Working Around an Existing House

F-6 was among the first sectors CDA developed, and its housing stock shows it. Many properties around Kohsar Market and the surrounding sub-sectors still sit on original 1960s and 1970s structures — load-bearing brick walls, shallow footings designed for a single storey, and services that have been patched repeatedly over five decades.

At the same time, F-6 land is among the most expensive in Islamabad. That combination produces a very specific kind of project: owners who want a modern home but are deciding whether to renovate the existing structure, extend it upward, or demolish and rebuild entirely. Al Sadat Builders does all three in F-6, and we start every one of them with a structural assessment rather than an assumption.

Key point: In F-6 we inspect the existing footings before recommending a second storey. A 1970s single-storey foundation is often not sized for the additional load, and strengthening it later is far more expensive than planning for it now.

Local Conditions

What Makes F-6 Projects Unusual

Old structures, high land value and mature street planting all shape what happens on site.

🔍

Assessing 1970s Load-Bearing Structures

Original F-6 houses use load-bearing brick walls and footings sized for one storey. Before we quote anything, we inspect the foundation depth, wall condition and whether an upward extension is structurally viable.

🧱

Renovate, Extend or Rebuild — Honestly Compared

With F-6 land values where they are, a rebuild is often the better investment — but not always. We put the structural findings and a cost comparison in writing so the decision is yours, not ours.

🌳

Mature Trees & Excavation Constraints

F-6 streets are heavily planted, and mature tree roots extend well past the trunk. Excavation and demolition need care to avoid destabilising street trees and neighbouring foundations.

📐

Setbacks Against an Existing Older Footprint

An existing house may sit closer to the boundary than current CDA setback rules allow. Rebuilding to today's rules can shrink your footprint compared to the house standing there now — we confirm this before design begins.

Approvals & Compliance

CDA Approvals in F-6

Older sectors often have a gap between the standing structure and today's rules.

  • Building plan approval from CDA Building Control is required for any rebuild or structural extension, based on a full architectural and structural drawing set.
  • Current setback lines and covered-area limits may be tighter than those the original house was built under — we confirm what applies to your plot before designing.
  • Structural design must satisfy Islamabad's seismic zone requirements and is subject to structural vetting.
  • Demolition of an existing structure follows CDA's procedure, including approvals and safe disposal of debris.
  • A completion certificate requires CDA inspection confirming the built structure matches the approved drawings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Construction Questions About F-6

Should I renovate or rebuild my old house in F-6?

It comes down to the condition of the original structure. If the footings are sound and the brickwork is not degrading, renovation or a controlled extension is usually the sensible investment. If the footings are undersized for anything beyond one storey, the services need complete replacement, and the walls are showing ongoing damp and cracks, then a rebuild often costs less over ten years than repeated repairs. We inspect first and give you the comparison in writing.

Can you add a second storey to my F-6 house?

Frequently yes, but it must be verified rather than assumed. We inspect the existing foundation and walls, and where the footings are insufficient we design the strengthening work alongside the extension. Adding a full storey onto a 1970s single-storey foundation without checking is how structural cracking starts.

Do you handle CDA approval for a rebuild in F-6?

Yes. We prepare the architectural drawings, structural design and services layout, coordinate the CDA submission, and build strictly to the approved set. One thing to plan for: current setback rules may require a smaller footprint than the existing house, so the rebuild design has to work within today's limits rather than the original ones.
