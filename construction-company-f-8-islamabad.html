F-8 Construction & Renovation

F-8 Mixes Large Residential Plots With an Institutional Edge

F-8 is best known for Faisal Mosque, and the sector's character reflects that proximity. It holds a mix of large residential plots alongside institutional and office use, particularly along Jinnah Avenue and around the F-8 Markaz. The residential blocks run from F-8/1 through F-8/4, with generous plot sizes and a quieter feel than F-7's commercial frontage.

That mix shapes the work. Plots here are large enough for substantial houses with wide spans and generous setbacks, so the structural design carries more weight than on a compact sector plot. At the same time, the presence of professional offices and institutional buildings nearby means site discipline — noise timing, dust control, access — matters more than usual. Al Sadat Builders plans for both.

Key point: On larger F-8 plots, wide spans and open plans are the norm. We design the column grid and beam depths for the loads they actually carry rather than adapting a standard layout.

Local Conditions

What Matters on an F-8 Project

Wide plots and a mixed-use neighbourhood change both the engineering and the working conditions.

📏

Wide Spans & Structural Design

F-8 plots allow genuinely open ground floors and large rooms. Those spans need engineered beam and column design — not a plan copied from a smaller sector and stretched.

🔇

Site Discipline Near Offices & Institutions

With professional and institutional buildings close by, noise timing, dust suppression and material movement need active management so the project does not generate complaints.

🏗️

Basement & Dewatering

Larger F-8 plots often support a basement. That requires shoring design, a dewatering plan for the water table and full external tanking rather than a surface-applied damp coat.

🌳

Setbacks & Landscape Integration

The generous setbacks F-8 allows are an asset. We plan the building line, plinth protection and drainage so landscaping can actually be built without compromising the waterproofing.

Approvals & Compliance

CDA Approvals in F-8

Larger plots and basement work mean a more detailed submission.

  • Building plan approval from CDA Building Control is required before construction, based on the complete architectural and structural drawing set.
  • Setback lines, covered-area ratio and height limits apply per plot and street width — we verify your specific plot before finalising the design.
  • Basements require a shoring and dewatering approach as part of the submission, not as a site decision after excavation begins.
  • Structural design must meet Islamabad's seismic zone requirements and is subject to structural vetting.
  • Completion certificates are issued after CDA inspection confirms the built structure matches the approved drawings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Construction Questions About F-8

Can you build a basement on an F-8 plot?

Yes — basements are common on larger F-8 plots and we build them regularly. The three critical elements are shoring design for the excavation, a real dewatering plan for the water table, and full external tanking on the basement walls and floor. A bitumen coat applied to the outside face is not a basement waterproofing system, and treating it as one is the single most common cause of damp basements in Islamabad.

What does construction cost in F-8?

F-8 prices sit near the top of the Islamabad range because plots are large, spans are wide and finishing expectations are high. We work from an itemised BOQ with named material brands rather than a flat square-foot rate, so each stage — structure, waterproofing, services, finishing — is costed and visible separately.

Do you renovate older F-8 houses as well as build new ones?

Yes. Many F-8 houses have sound structures but outdated services, layouts and finishes. We assess what can be retained, then modernise the wiring, plumbing, kitchens, bathrooms and finishes, and add a second storey where the existing footings will carry the load.
